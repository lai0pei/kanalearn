46 core sounds. Tap a tile to hear it. Use the ▶ button at the start of a row to hear the whole row.

Voiced Sounds — Dakuten & Handakuten (濁音・半濁音)

Two small marks change the sound: ゛(dakuten) voices it (k→g, s→z, t→d, h→b); ゜(handakuten) turns h→p.

Combined Sounds — Yōon (拗音)

A small ゃ/ゅ/ょ (ャ/ュ/ョ) merges with the syllable before it into a single beat: き + ゃ = きゃ (kya).

Particles & What They Mean

Some hiragana double as grammar particles — tiny words that follow a noun and show its role in the sentence. Particles are always written in hiragana, never katakana.

How Japanese writing fits together

Three scripts in one sentence

私はコーヒーを飲みます
watashi wa kōhī o nomimasu — “I drink coffee.”
Kanji (私, 飲) = word roots · hiragana (は, を, みます) = grammar · katakana (コーヒー) = loanword.

What katakana is for

Foreign loanwords and names, sound effects, emphasis (like italics), and scientific names of plants and animals.

Furigana

Small hiragana printed above or beside a kanji to show how it is read — common in children’s books and manga.

Where kana come from

Both scripts grew out of kanji. Hiragana are cursive kanji; katakana are pieces of kanji. か and カ both come from 加; あ comes from 安 and ア from 阿.

Reading & pronunciation rules

One kana = one beat

Japanese is timed by beats (mora). とうきょう (Tōkyō) is 4 beats: to·u·kyo·u. Small ゃ/ゅ/ょ join the previous beat; っ, ん and long vowels each take a full beat.

Small っ / ッ (sokuon)

きって kitte — a small tsu is a one-beat pause that doubles the next consonant. Compare きて kite. In katakana: ベッド beddo.

Long vowels in hiragana

Add a vowel: おばあさん obaasan, おじいさん ojiisan. o-sounds usually take う (こうこう kōkō), sometimes お (おおきい ōkii). e-sounds often take い (せんせい sensei).

Long vowels in katakana

Use the dash ー: コーヒー kōhī, ケーキ kēki. In vertical text the dash is written vertically.

は / へ / を as particles

は is read wa, へ is read e, を is read o when they are particles. わたしは watashi wa, にほんへ nihon e.

ん changes with the next sound

Sounds like “m” before m/b/p (さんぽ sanpo), like “ng” before k/g (りんご ringo). Always one full beat: ほん = ho·n.

Whispered vowels

i and u often go quiet between voiceless consonants or at the end: です sounds like “dess”, すき like “ski”.

Pitch, not stress

Japanese uses high/low pitch. In Tokyo speech はし HA-shi (high-low) = chopsticks, ha-SHI (low-high) = bridge. Context usually makes it clear.

ぢ・づ

Sound the same as じ (ji) and ず (zu). じ/ず are the normal spellings; ぢ/づ appear mainly when ち/つ becomes voiced in a compound (はな + ち → はなぢ) or after the same sound (つづく).

The r sound

Japanese r is a light tongue tap — somewhere between English r, l and d. Don’t curl the tongue.

Writing rules

Basic stroke order

Top → bottom, left → right. A horizontal line usually comes before the vertical that crosses it (た, す, せ). Tap any tile to watch the animation.

Small kana

ゃ ゅ ょ っ (ャ ュ ョ ッ) are written about half size, sitting low in their square.

Dakuten ゛ and handakuten ゜

Always go at the top-right, written last: か → が, は → ば → ぱ.

Hiragana vs katakana feel

Hiragana are round and flowing; katakana are straight and angular. Keep katakana strokes crisp and short.

Extra katakana for foreign sounds

Small ァィゥェォ build sounds Japanese doesn’t have: ファン fan, パーティー pātī (party), ディズニー Dizunī, シェフ shefu (chef), ウェブ webu (web).

Practice tip

Say the sound out loud each time you write it. Use the practice pad with the guide on, then turn the guide off and try from memory.

Look-alikes to watch

シ vs ツ

シ (shi): short strokes stacked on the left, long stroke sweeps up. ツ (tsu): short strokes along the top, long stroke sweeps down.

ソ vs ン

ソ (so): short stroke upright, long stroke sweeps down. ン (n): short stroke flatter, long stroke sweeps up.

ク / ケ / タ

ケ has a horizontal line reaching out to the right. タ has an extra small stroke inside. ク has neither.

ウ / ワ / フ

ウ has a small tick on top. ワ has the left vertical but no tick. フ is a single stroke.

コ / ユ, ノ / メ, ヌ / ス

ユ’s bottom line sticks out past the side. メ is ノ with a crossing stroke. ヌ is like ス with an extra crossing stroke.

チ vs テ

チ starts with a slanted stroke on top. テ has two straight horizontal lines on top.

ぬ / め, る / ろ

ぬ and る end with a small loop. め and ろ don’t.

れ / わ / ね

All share the same left part. ね ends in a loop, れ ends with a kick outward, わ ends with a smooth inward curve.

は / ほ, き / さ

ほ has two horizontal lines on the right, は has one. き has two horizontal lines, さ has one.

あ / お, い / り

お has a separate dot on the right. り’s right stroke is long and drops down; い’s right stroke is short.

Katakana vs kanji twins

カ/力 (power), エ/工 (craft), ニ/二 (two), ハ/八 (eight), ロ/口 (mouth) look almost identical — the surrounding words tell you which it is.
