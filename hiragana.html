Voiced Sounds — Dakuten & Handakuten (濁音・半濁音)

Two small marks change the sound: ゙ (dakuten) voices it, ゚ (handakuten) turns h- into p-.

Combined Sounds — Yōon (拗音)

A small ゃ/ゅ/ょ (or ャ/ュ/ョ) fuses with the previous syllable into one beat.

Particles & What They Mean

These single hiragana do double duty as grammar particles — tiny words that glue a Japanese sentence together. Click one to hear it.

Possession — works like 's or “of” in English.
私（わたし）の本（ほん） (watashi no hon) — “my book”
(pronounced “wa”)Topic marker — written は but read “wa” when used this way. Marks what the sentence is about.
私は学生です (watashi wa gakusei desu) — “I am a student (lit. as for me, [I] am a student)”
(pronounced “o”)Direct object marker — written を but read “o”. Marks the thing an action is done to.
本を読む (hon o yomu) — “read a book”
Direction, target or time — “to / at / in / onto”.
学校に行く (gakkou ni iku) — “go to school”
Location of an action or the means used — “at / by / with”.
図書館で勉強する (toshokan de benkyou suru) — “study at the library”
“And” (joining nouns) or “with” (a companion).
友達と行く (tomodachi to iku) — “go with a friend”
“Also / too” — replaces は or が on the word it follows.
私も行く (watashi mo iku) — “I am going too”
Question marker — added to the end of a sentence to ask something.
元気ですか (genki desu ka) — “Are you doing well?”
Subject marker — identifies who or what is doing something, or what exists/is liked.
猫が好きです (neko ga suki desu) — “I like cats (lit. cats are likable)”
